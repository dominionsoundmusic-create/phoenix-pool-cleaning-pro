Mesa's Water and What It Does to Your Pool

Mesa draws its water from a mix of Salt River Project surface water and city-operated wells. The blend varies by season and neighborhood, but calcium hardness typically runs 250 to 500 ppm — well above the 200 ppm ceiling that equipment manufacturers recommend. East Mesa neighborhoods on heavier well supply tend to run harder than areas closer to the Consolidated Canal that receive more surface water.

That calcium builds up everywhere the water touches — waterline tile, heater heat exchangers, salt cell plates, and pump internals. A pool that looks fine on the surface can be silently scaling its equipment underneath. Weekly chemistry testing is the only way to track the saturation index and adjust before the calcium tips from dissolved into deposited.

What Every Weekly Visit Covers

Same checklist, every week. Skim the surface, empty baskets, brush walls and tile, vacuum the floor. Test chlorine, pH, alkalinity, cyanuric acid, and calcium hardness — adjust what needs adjusting. Check the filter pressure, inspect the pump, and look at every piece of visible equipment. If something is off, you hear about it the same day.
