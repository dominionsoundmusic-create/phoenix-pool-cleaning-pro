This page sets out what is true about Apache Junction pools, what the city's own pool rules say, and how one free call reaches a pool company that works here.

Apache Junction: an East Valley city in two counties

Most people think of Apache Junction as a Pinal County town, and most of it is. The U.S. Census Bureau's boundary map lists the city in both Maricopa and Pinal counties, and a downtown plan prepared for the city describes it as lying mostly within Pinal. The 2020 census counted 38,499 residents. Lost Dutchman State Park sits at the foot of the Superstition Mountains on the Apache Trail, State Route 88, and the rock wall of the Superstitions is the skyline for a large part of town.

Part of the town lives here only for the cooler months, and some homes sit empty through the summer. For pools, that seasonal pattern matters: a pool whose owners leave in spring faces the hottest months of the year with nobody at home to notice a stopped pump.

Pool service in Apache Junction, AZ: what owners ask for

The steady need is weekly pool cleaning and maintenance, which keeps chlorine, pH and stabilizer in range and gets someone looking at the equipment every week, whether or not the owners are in town. Hard fill water leaves a white line on the tile over time, which is what pool tile cleaning removes. Dust from the open desert loads filters fast, so pool filter cleaning comes up often after a storm, and a pump that hums or loses prime points to pool pump repair. A pool that turned while the owners were away belongs with green pool cleanup, and an older, rough finish leads to pool resurfacing.

Two water providers, one hard supply

The City of Apache Junction lists two water providers, the Apache Junction Water District and Arizona Water Company, and which one serves you depends on your address. Your water bill tells you. Both publish an annual water quality report.

Sodium is one part of the total dissolved solids a pool inherits.

Hardness is mostly dissolved calcium and magnesium, and it is not a health problem in drinking water. In a pool it is the raw material for scale. One grain per gallon equals about 17.1 milligrams per liter, so the district's 10.5 grain threshold is roughly 180 milligrams per liter.

What that water and this climate do to a pool here

The Arizona Department of Water Resources says a standard uncovered backyard pool in the state loses four to six feet of water a year to evaporation, most of it in summer. Only the water leaves. The calcium and salts stay, and every top off adds a fresh dose from the hose. Over a few summers the calcium hardness and total dissolved solids (TDS, everything dissolved in the water) climb well past the tap water level. A careful pool technician tracks this with the Langelier Saturation Index (LSI), a calculation that combines pH, alkalinity, calcium and water temperature to show whether the water leans toward scale or toward etching plaster. Hot water tips it toward scale.

Arizona State Parks describes summer days at Lost Dutchman as climbing above 100 degrees. At those temperatures chlorine burns off quickly in the sun, which is why cyanuric acid, the stabilizer that shields chlorine from ultraviolet light, matters so much. Stabilizer only leaves the pool when water leaves it, so it builds up too. When the minerals and stabilizer get too high to balance, the reset is a partial drain and refill, covered in the guide to how often to drain a pool in Arizona.

If you leave for the summer

A pool can go from clear to green fast in desert heat once the chlorine is gone, and a tripped breaker or a timer that reset after a power blip can stop the pump for days. Before you head out for the season, arrange regular service with a pool company and agree on how it will tell you about problems. Leave the company a way into the yard, the breaker location and your contact details, and tell a neighbor who is staying. Keep gates latched: a green pool is a drowning hazard because nobody can see the bottom, and children should never be near an unfenced or uncovered pool without an adult.

  • Baskets. Empty the skimmer and pump baskets before you go.
  • Timer. Confirm the pump schedule and write it down for the company.
  • Filter gauge. Note the pressure reading on a clean filter so a later reading means something.
  • Breaker. Reset a tripped pool breaker or GFCI only once. If it trips again, leave it off and call a professional.
  • Chemicals. Never mix pool chemicals with each other. Store them apart, dry, shaded and out of children's reach, and leave acid work to a professional.

Monsoon dust on the east edge of the Valley

The National Weather Service treats June 15 through September 30 as monsoon season. Apache Junction has open desert around it, and dust storms show up on the air quality monitor in town. Pinal County's draft report on 2025 particulate exceedances lists July 1, 2025, as a day when the Apache Junction monitor reached a daily Air Quality Index of 208 for coarse dust, in the unhealthy range, during the summer monsoon pattern.

For a pool, a storm like that means silt on the floor, leaves and grit in the skimmer, a filter that loads up in an afternoon and chlorine used up by everything that blew in. After the storm passes and 30 minutes have gone by since the last thunder, empty the baskets, check that the pump is running and test the water before anyone swims. The monsoon pool care guide has the full before and after checklist.

Apache Junction pool rules: fences, permits and drained water

The city publishes its own guidance on pools and pool barriers. It points to City Code Section 7-4 for swimming pool enclosures and says the property owner, and anyone in charge of the pool, must keep the enclosure in good working order, including gates, doors, locks and latches. Nobody may remove or alter part of the enclosure except to repair or replace it in line with the code. The barrier rules in force when a pool was built still apply to it.

On permits, the same guidance treats re-plastering as general maintenance that needs no permit or inspection, and says re-plastering does not change which barrier rules apply. That is useful to know if a company recommends resurfacing; it does not change the need to check the contractor's license.

Where pool water can go. Apache Junction's stormwater management program lists pool and spa water from backwashing or emptying as an allowed discharge to the storm drain system only when no chemicals, chlorine included, have been added for at least three days before the release. Other discharges are treated as possible illicit discharges until shown otherwise, and the city's stormwater standards allow a civil fine of up to $2,500, with repeat violations prosecuted as a misdemeanor. Questions and reports go to the Engineering Division at (480) 474-5084. Ask the pool company exactly where drained or backwashed water will go before the hose comes out.

Electricity in Apache Junction comes from SRP (Salt River Project), and natural gas from Southwest Gas, according to the city's utility page. Neither repairs pool equipment. If the house has power but the pump is silent, that is a pump, motor or wiring question for a pool company. If you ever smell gas near a pool heater, leave the area, do not touch switches or the heater, and from a safe distance call 911 or Southwest Gas (opens in a new tab).
