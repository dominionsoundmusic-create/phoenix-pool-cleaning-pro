A filter that is packed with dust from the last storm, oils from swimmers and fine mineral grit from hard water forces the pump to work harder and lets cloudy water win. Cartridge, DE or sand: how each filter traps dirt

Every pool filter does the same job: water from the pump passes through a material that holds back dirt and lets clean water return to the pool. What differs is the material, how fine a particle it can catch, and how it gets cleaned. Hayward, a filter maker, and Pool & Spa News, a trade publication for pool technicians, describe the three types in the terms below. Micron ratings are approximate. A micron is one thousandth of a millimeter, and particles under about 10 microns are the ones that make water look hazy.

Sand and DE filters send pool water out to waste each time they are backwashed, and that water has to be replaced, which matters in a desert where every gallon is refilled from the tap. Cartridge filters avoid backwashing, which is one reason many Valley pools use them. The tradeoff is that cartridges have to be taken out and rinsed by hand.

Which pool filter is best for a Phoenix pool?

There is no single answer. A careful pool technician looks at three things before suggesting a change. First, the pump's flow rate: Hayward notes that a filter that keeps running high pressure with weak return flow may simply be smaller than the pump it is paired with. Second, how the pool is used, since heavy swimming and lots of sunscreen load a filter faster. Third, how much handling you can live with. A DE filter gives the clearest water but needs fresh powder after every backwash and careful disposal of the spent powder. If someone recommends a new filter, ask why the current one cannot be repaired and ask to see the pump and filter ratings side by side. The pool equipment repair page covers the rest of the pad.

What a professional pool filter cleaning service involves

The steps differ by filter. Each starts the same way: pump off, pressure released, and a look at the gauge before and after so you can see the difference.

Cartridge filter clean

The technician turns the pump off at the timer or automation, opens the air relief valve to bleed pressure, then removes the tank lid and lifts out the cartridges. Each element is hosed pleat by pleat and checked for tears, flattened pleats, cracked end caps and a soft or crushed core. If oils or mineral scale are baked into the fabric, a soak in a cleaner made for filters may follow. The tank O-ring is inspected and lubricated, the lid is reseated, air is bled, and the clean starting pressure is written down.

DE filter clean

A DE filter is usually backwashed first, then opened so the grid assembly can come out. Each grid is hosed and checked, especially along the seams where the fabric meets the frame, because even a small opening lets powder through. The manifold and standpipe O-ring are checked for cracks. After the filter is closed and running, the technician adds the correct amount of fresh DE for that filter's size through the skimmer, since too much powder can bridge between the grids.

Sand filter service

Routine sand care is a backwash and a rinse setting on the multiport valve. A fuller service checks whether the bed has packed into channels, which lets water slip past the sand, and whether sand is showing up on the pool floor, a sign of a broken lateral. Hayward advises replacing the sand when backwashing no longer brings the pressure down, or when the time between backwashes shrinks a lot.

On any filter, a careful technician also looks at the things that make a filter clog early: low chlorine, algae starting in corners, a pump basket that is cracked and letting leaves through, or new plaster still shedding dust. Pentair notes that plaster dust can make filter runs very short until it is gone.

Backwash and rinse water: where it may go in Phoenix

Dirty filter water is not just water. It carries dust, organic debris, chlorine and, for DE filters, spent powder. Cities in the Valley treat it as something to keep out of the street and the storm drain, because storm drains run untreated to washes and rivers. Rules differ from city to city, so check your own city's page before anyone backwashes.

For the City of Phoenix, the Water Services Department tells residents to reuse backwash water on landscaping on their own property or to send larger amounts down the home's sanitary sewer cleanout, and not to drain into the street, an alley or other city right-of-way. Under City Code Chapter 32C, pool water may not go to the storm drain. Phoenix allows clean, dechlorinated water to reach the right-of-way only by permit and only for homes with no sewer cleanout, and it lists pool backwash as prohibited from that route.

Mesa spells out DE handling in more detail. Its drain and backwash page recommends a separation tank for DE and cellulose fiber filters to capture the powder, and says spent DE can be dried, bagged and put in the trash. Mesa also says cartridge rinse water should go over gravel, lawn or another planted area. DE powder is a dust to respect. Diatomaceous earth used in pool filters can contain crystalline silica, and NIOSH links breathing respirable crystalline silica to serious lung disease. Do not pour or sweep dry DE where people breathe it, keep the bag away from children and pets, and read the product's safety data sheet. Many homeowners leave DE handling to their pool company for this reason.

Pool filter pressure: low, high and what to check

The round gauge on top of the filter tank is the best tool you have. After a filter is freshly cleaned and the pump is at its normal speed, note the reading. That is your clean starting pressure. Hayward advises cleaning or backwashing when pressure rises about 8 to 10 psi above that number. The filter cleaning schedule guide explains how to use that rise to plan cleanings by season.

Safety at the filter. Pentair warns that trapped air can pressurize a filter enough to blow the lid off. Never loosen a filter clamp or lid while the pump is running. Turn the pump off and open the air relief valve first, and if you are unsure, leave it closed and call. Do not open motor covers or electrical boxes. If a breaker or GFCI has tripped, you can reset it once; if it trips again, stop and call a pool technician.

Signs a cartridge or DE grid needs replacing

Cleaning brings a filter back only so far. Pentair's cartridge filter manual expects an average media life of about three years under normal conditions, and Hayward says to replace a cartridge that is damaged, broken, cracked or not attached securely. Pool & Spa News adds that letting pressure climb too high before cleaning can collapse pleats and ruin an element early.

  • Torn, frayed or flattened pleats on a cartridge, or end caps that are cracked or pulling away.
  • A clean starting pressure that keeps creeping up after each cleaning, even when the cartridge looks clean.
  • DE coming back into the pool, which Hayward and Pentair tie to torn grid fabric or a cracked manifold.
  • Sand on the pool floor or very short runs between backwashes on a sand filter.
  • Water that stays hazy with good chlorine and normal run times.

Why filters work harder in the Valley

Phoenix pools face a mix that many pools elsewhere do not. City of Phoenix tap water is hard: its 2025 water quality report lists total hardness of roughly 10 to 17.6 grains per gallon, which is 172 to 302 parts per million. Calcium and magnesium in that water can leave scale on surfaces and in filter fabric as water evaporates in the heat and is topped off again. Summer storms add a different load. The National Weather Service's official monsoon season runs June 15 to September 30, and its Phoenix office describes the thunderstorm-driven dust walls called haboobs, which can drop a fine layer of dirt into every open pool in a neighborhood. The monsoon pool care guide covers what to do before and after those storms.

A pool that is not filtered for long enough turns green, and a green pool is a safety problem as much as a cleaning one: the bottom cannot be seen, and children should be kept well away from it. Standing green water can also breed mosquitoes; Maricopa County Environmental Services takes mosquito and green pool complaints at 602-506-6616. If it has already turned, the green pool cleanup page explains the recovery.

Questions to ask the pool company

  • Is filter cleaning included in a weekly plan, and how often, or is it a separate visit?
  • What was the pressure before and after, and what should my clean starting pressure be?
  • Did you see torn pleats, grid damage or broken laterals? Can I see them?
  • Where will the backwash or rinse water go, and how will spent DE be handled?
  • If you recommend a new cartridge, grid set or filter, what is the written price for each option?
