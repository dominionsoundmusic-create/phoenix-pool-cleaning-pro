Why this line exists

Finding a pool company in a hurry is harder than it should be. You search, you get a wall of ads, you leave voicemails, and the pool keeps turning greener while you wait.

Phoenix Pool Cleaning Pro is a simple fix for that: one free number, answered by an automated assistant at any hour, that transfers you to an independent, locally owned pool service company working in your part of the Valley. One number covers weekly cleaning, green pool cleanups, pumps, filters, heaters, tile, leaks and resurfacing, from Anthem to Maricopa and from Buckeye to Apache Junction.

Alongside the phone line, this site publishes guides written for desert pools: what hard water does to tile and heaters, how monsoon dust affects the filter, what pool service tends to cost, and how to check a contractor before you sign. Every guide lists its sources so you can check them.

What makes the line different

Free to call

You never pay the line anything. Any price you hear comes from the pool company, which bills you directly.

Answered 24/7

An automated assistant picks up every call, day and night, weekends and holidays. When the company is tied up, it gets your details and returns the call.

Local company first

The call goes to an independent, locally owned Phoenix-area pool company, which hears a short summary of the problem before it picks up.

Help checking who you hire

The site explains which pool work generally needs an Arizona ROC license and how to look a company up on the ROC contractor search, so you can check before work starts.

What the line will never claim

Plenty of pool websites are full of badges and promises. This one is not, on purpose. Because Phoenix Pool Cleaning Pro does not do pool work, it will never tell you that it cleans or repairs pools, that it has technicians or trucks, or that it holds a license. It will never say the companies it connects you with are licensed, insured, certified or highly rated, because it does not verify those things. It does not publish reviews, invent prices, or name the companies it works with.

What it will do is tell you plainly how the arrangement works: the companies may pay the line a referral fee for calls, and that is disclosed in the footer of every page. The terms of use and privacy policy spell out the rest.
