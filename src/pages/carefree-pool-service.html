Carefree: a small desert town with a water company of its own

Carefree sits in the desert foothills north of Scottsdale, next door to Cave Creek. Its water is handled by the Carefree Water Company, which town materials describe as wholly owned by a Utilities Community Facilities District whose board members are the seven elected members of the Town Council. In other words, the people who set town policy also oversee the water.

The company does not treat most of its own surface water. The town's water fact sheets explain that it holds a Central Arizona Project allocation and has "treat and transport" agreements with Scottsdale and Cave Creek, so CAP water is treated by a neighboring system and delivered to Carefree. The same materials describe the local groundwater aquifer as healthy but very limited in size, which is why the company leans mainly on CAP water while also operating local wells.

What Carefree's water setup means for a pool

  • The sourceMainly Colorado River water delivered by the CAP, treated by a neighboring system under the town's agreements, with a small local groundwater supply.
  • The ownerThe Carefree Water Company, owned by a district governed by the elected Town Council.
  • The historyParts of town were once served by Cave Creek's system, and the town has worked to bring all of Carefree under its own water company.
  • The hardnessAsk the water company for its latest annual water quality report. Neighboring Cave Creek, also on CAP water, reported 12 to 15 grains per gallon in 2018.

For a pool, the details of who treats the water matter less than what is dissolved in it. CAP water is hard, and in the dry desert air a pool loses water to evaporation every day. Each top off brings in more calcium, which shows up as crust at the waterline and scale inside a heater or salt cell. A pool company that tests calcium hardness, pH and alkalinity together, and adjusts toward balance, keeps that buildup slow.

Pools among boulders and native desert

A natural desert yard is beautiful, and it changes the work. Three things a careful pool technician watches on a Carefree lot:

What falls in

Native trees drop leaves, flowers and seed pods, and wind carries dust and plant litter off the surrounding desert. Skimmer baskets and filters fill faster than in a walled yard with a lawn.

Where the rain runs

On a sloped lot, storm runoff can carry soil and gravel toward the pool. After a heavy rain, the water may need extra cleaning and a chemistry check.

Rock features

Waterfalls and boulder edges look natural, but every crevice is a place for algae and scale to start. They need brushing and a close look for leaks where rock meets plaster.

Leaving a Carefree pool for the summer

If you spend part of the year away, the pool keeps working without you. Heat, sun and the monsoon season, which the National Weather Service sets as June 15 to September 30, can turn a clear pool green in a short time when chlorine runs out or the pump stops. A neglected pool also becomes a mosquito breeding site, which Maricopa County's vector control program investigates. Before you leave:

  1. Set up service first. Arrange regular visits before you go, not after a neighbor calls about the water.
  2. Make access easy. Agree on how the company enters the yard and that gates are closed and latched after every visit.
  3. Agree on repairs. Decide what the company may fix without calling you and what needs your approval first, and get its prices in writing.
  4. Leave the timer right. Confirm the pump schedule and that the equipment area is clear of debris.
  5. Plan for storms. Ask what the company does after a dust storm or heavy rain while you are away.

Safety basics. Never mix pool chemicals with each other, and keep them stored apart, dry, shaded and away from children. If you smell gas near a pool heater, leave the area, do not touch switches or the heater, and from a safe distance call your gas utility's emergency number or 911. Keep children away from any pool that is unfenced, uncovered or too cloudy to see the bottom.
