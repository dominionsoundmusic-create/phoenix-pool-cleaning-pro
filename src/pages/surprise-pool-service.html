Surprise: one city, several water companies

The City of Surprise provides water, sewer and trash service, yet its own water page is clear that not every resident or business inside the city is served by the city. EPCOR, a private utility, provides water to certain Surprise residents under a Certificate of Convenience and Necessity issued by the Arizona Corporation Commission, and EPCOR treats Surprise as its own rate district. When Surprise held an election on EPCOR's franchise, the city described EPCOR as one of several public water utility companies that, along with the city, provide water or sewer service to some Surprise residents.

So two pools a few miles apart in Surprise may be filled from different systems. Before you trust any rule of thumb about "West Valley water," look at the name on your water bill. Then pull that utility's annual water quality report and read the hardness and total dissolved solids lines. A good pool technician will want those numbers, or will test your fill water from the hose bib, before deciding how to balance the pool.

What the city's water report says about hardness

The city publishes a water quality report for each system it runs. Its 2022 report for the Mountain Vista system, which federal drinking water records list as a groundwater system serving roughly 40,000 people, gave these mineral readings:

Mineral readings from the City of Surprise Mountain Vista 2022 water quality report
ReadingRange in the 2022 reportWhy a pool owner cares
Total hardness43 to 91 ppm (5.3 grains per gallon at the high end)All the dissolved calcium and magnesium. It is what turns into white crust at the waterline.
Calcium hardness23 to 50 ppmThe calcium part alone. Pool water needs some calcium; too little or too much both cause trouble.
Total dissolved solids226 to 300 ppmEverything dissolved in the water. It climbs in a pool as water evaporates and chemicals are added.

Those numbers are low next to what many Valley pool owners are used to hearing. That does not make a Surprise pool scale free. A pool loses water to the sun and wind all summer, and evaporation takes only the water, never the minerals. Every top off adds a fresh dose of calcium and solids, and the chemicals added each week add more. Over a few years, the water in the pool can end up far harder than the water from the tap.

Soft fill water raises a second point. When calcium hardness in a pool runs too low, the water can pull calcium out of plaster and grout instead of laying it down. That is why a careful technician tests calcium hardness in the pool itself, not just pH and chlorine, and balances the water as a whole. If your address is on EPCOR or another utility, its report will show different numbers, so check your own. The city also says its newest reports live on its Drinking Water Quality Reports page.

Draining or backwashing a pool in Surprise

The City of Surprise publishes its own guidance on where pool and spa water may go. It is stricter than many people expect, and it applies whether you drain the pool yourself or a company does it for you.

Where it may not go

City ordinance rules bar pool water from the street, curb, sidewalk and neighbors' yards, and from anywhere else off your property. Filter backwash may never go into the street, curb, sidewalk, a retention basin or the private property next door.

Option one: your own yard

The city's first choice is to let the water soak into rock, desert landscaping or another part of your lot, but only if it stays there. It must not flood a neighbor or run off into a ditch, street or storm drain.

Option two: the sewer cleanout

The other route is the sanitary sewer, through the cleanout on your property. The city describes it as a black threaded cap with a raised square nut, usually at ground level beside the house. Call the city at 623.222.6200 with questions.

The city's reason is practical: water that runs off a lot picks up debris and collects in park retention basins, where it can be a health risk to children and pets. If you are paying a company to drain the pool, ask which of the two options it plans to use and how it will keep sand and debris out of the sewer line.

Pool service prices in Surprise and what to ask

People often search for pool service prices in Surprise, AZ before they call anyone. The line cannot give you a price, because it does not do the work, and the companies set their own rates. What you can do is ask the same questions of every company so the quotes line up. The pool service cost guide walks through the published ranges and why real quotes vary from house to house.

  • What happens on each visit? Skimming, brushing, baskets, vacuuming and a water test should be spelled out.
  • Are chemicals part of the price or billed on top? Get the answer in writing either way.
  • Do you test calcium hardness and total dissolved solids, or only chlorine and pH? With soft city water and heavy evaporation, both matter.
  • Who cleans the filter, and how often? The filter is part of the price question too.
  • If I need a repair, what is your ROC license number? Then look it up yourself before work begins.
  • If the pool has to be drained, where will the water go? The answer should match the city's two options above.

Safe checks before you call

  • Baskets. Empty the skimmer and pump baskets with the pump off.
  • Water level. If the level has dropped below the middle of the skimmer opening, the pump can pull air. Note how fast it is dropping.
  • Filter gauge. Write down the pressure reading so you can tell the company.
  • Timer. Make sure the timer or automation still runs the pump on schedule after a power blip.
  • Breaker. Reset a tripped GFCI or breaker once. If it trips again, leave it off and call.

Safety first. Never mix pool chemicals with each other. Store them apart, dry, shaded and out of children's reach, and follow the label. Leave acid work to a professional. A green or cloudy pool is a drowning hazard because you cannot see the bottom, so keep children away from it and never leave a child alone near water. If you smell gas near a pool heater, leave the area, do not touch switches or the heater, and from a safe distance call the gas company on your bill or 911.
