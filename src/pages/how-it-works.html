Phoenix Pool Cleaning Pro exists so that you only need one number for a pool problem, whether it is weekly cleaning, a green pool, a pump that will not prime or a crusted tile line. Here is exactly what happens when you dial {{ site.business.phone_display }}, and what the line does not do.

  1. An automated assistant answers.Every call is picked up by an automated assistant, 24 hours a day and 7 days a week, including weekends and holidays. There is no menu to dig through.
  2. It asks what is going on with the pool.Say it in plain words: the water is cloudy, the pump hums but does not run, the heater clicks and stops, the water level keeps dropping, or you want someone to take over weekly cleaning. It also asks which part of the Valley you are in.
  3. Your call is transferred to a local pool company.The assistant hands the call to an independent, locally owned pool service company that works in your area. Before it picks up, that company hears a short summary, so you do not have to start from the beginning.
  4. If nobody can answer, you get a call back.When the company cannot pick up at that moment, your name, callback number, area and pool problem are taken and passed along so the company can call you back.
  5. You and the company take it from there.The company looks at the pool, explains what it found, quotes the work, does it and bills you directly. Whether you hire it is entirely your decision.

What the line does, and what it never does

The line is a connection, nothing more. Knowing where its job ends helps you ask the right questions of the company you reach.

What the line does

  • Answers every call, any hour, through an automated assistant
  • Notes what the pool problem is and where you are
  • Transfers you to an independent local pool service company
  • Passes your details along for a call back if the company cannot answer
  • Publishes guides, including how to look up a contractor on the Arizona ROC search

What the line never does

  • Clean, repair, inspect or test a pool
  • Quote a price, set a schedule or send anyone out
  • Charge you, or add anything to a company's bill
  • Check licenses or vouch for a company's work
  • Hold a contractor license of its own

What to have ready before you call

You do not need any of this to call, but it helps the pool company size up the job on the first conversation and helps you compare answers.

  • The symptom, in your words. What the water looks like, what the equipment is doing or not doing, and when it started.
  • Your general area. The town or neighborhood, so the call reaches a company that works there.
  • The equipment you have. Chlorine or salt system, cartridge, sand or DE filter, gas heater or heat pump, if you know. A phone photo of the equipment pad is useful later.
  • The filter pressure reading. If the gauge on top of the filter is easy to see, note the number.
  • Anything recent. A dust storm, a power outage, new plaster, a long trip away, or a recent repair.

Questions worth asking the company you reach

The company is independent, so a few minutes of questions protect you. These work for anything from weekly cleaning to a repair:

  • What does a visit include, and who supplies the chemicals? Get it in writing for recurring service.
  • What does the first visit or diagnosis cost? Ask before anyone drives out.
  • What is your Arizona ROC license number? For repairs, equipment replacement, leak work and resurfacing, then look it up yourself.
  • Can I have the quote in writing before work starts? With parts, labor and any warranty listed.
  • What should I do, or not do, until you arrive? For example, whether to keep the pump running.
