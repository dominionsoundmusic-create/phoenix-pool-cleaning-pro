Sun City: private pools with their own needs

Sun City has community pools, but a pool behind your own house is a different matter. A private pool is your responsibility from the water to the equipment pad. In the Valley's heat, that means steady chlorine, regular brushing and a pump that runs every day, even when no one is swimming.

Water comes from EPCOR, which runs the Sun City water system (state system number AZ0407099) and publishes a water quality report for it every year. Look for the hardness line in the latest report. Hard water leaves calcium scale on tile and inside heaters and salt cells, and because pools lose water to evaporation and get topped off, the minerals add up over time.

Pool care while you are away

If you spend part of the year somewhere else, the pool keeps running without you. A few arrangements before you leave save a lot of trouble when you get back.

Keep weekly service going

Heat and sun burn off chlorine quickly, and a pool can turn green between visits. Ask for a steady weekly schedule rather than pausing service while you travel.

Sort out gate access

Decide how the pool company gets into the yard, who else has a key, and that the gate latches behind every visit. A gate left open is a safety risk for any child nearby.

Agree on what to do about problems

Tell the company how to reach you, and set a dollar amount it may spend on a repair without calling first. Anything above that waits for your approval.

Pool service in Sun City, AZ: keeping pool chores safe

Some pool jobs are easy to hand off, and handing them off is often the safer choice. Lifting heavy chemical jugs, leaning out over the water with a long pole and working on a hot deck in summer are where falls, strains and heat illness happen. A pool service company can carry that load, and these habits help for anything you still do.

  • Work early or late. Do any pool chore in the cool of the morning or evening, drink water and stop if you feel dizzy or overheated.
  • Watch your footing. Wet decks and pool steps get slick. A handrail at the steps and nonslip surfaces help.
  • Leave the heavy lifting. Let the pool company carry and handle chemical containers.
  • Never mix pool chemicals with each other. Store them apart, dry, shaded and out of reach of visiting grandchildren, and follow the label.
  • Plan for young visitors. Never leave a child unsupervised near the water. Keep gates latched and doors to the pool area closed, and consider a pool alarm or safety cover.

Safe checks before you call

  • Look at the water. Cloudy or green water, or a bottom you cannot see, means stay out and keep others out.
  • Check the baskets from the deck. Empty the skimmer basket if you can reach it easily.
  • Read the filter gauge. Write down the number and share it on the call.
  • Check the timer. Confirm the pump is set to run, especially after a power outage.

Smell gas near a pool heater? Leave the area. Do not touch the heater or any switch. From a safe distance, call 911 or the gas company named on your bill.
