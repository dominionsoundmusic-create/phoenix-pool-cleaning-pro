Pool service in Arizona's biggest metro is local work. The water in Scottsdale is not the same as the water in Surprise, a Sun City backyard is not a Queen Creek lot, and each city writes its own rules about where pool water may drain.

The line answers calls from anywhere in the Phoenix metro, mostly in Maricopa County plus San Tan Valley, Apache Junction and the city of Maricopa in Pinal County. It has no offices: it transfers your call to an independent pool company that takes calls for your area. The pages below explain what is different about each place, with sources.

What changes from one town to the next

  • The water you fill with. Cities blend river water from the Salt and Verde, Colorado River water from the Central Arizona Project and groundwater in different amounts, so hardness differs.
  • Where pool water may go. Cities set their own rules for draining and backwashing; many send it to the sewer cleanout or your own yard and never to the street.
  • Pool barrier rules. Fence and gate requirements are set locally.
  • Utilities. Electricity comes from APS or SRP depending on the address, which matters when a storm knocks out power to the pump.
