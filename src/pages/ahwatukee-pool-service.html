Ahwatukee: a ranch name, a 1970s plan and a Phoenix village

The name came from a ranch. In 1970 a homebuilder bought about 2,080 acres on the far side of South Mountain, a parcel that took in the old Ahwatukee Ranch. Maricopa County approved the plans in November 1971, and 17 model homes opened near 50th Street and Elliot Road in 1973. Sales took off from there, and at the 1984 peak more than one house a day was selling. Part of the land had been a truck testing ground until it was sold for development in 1983.

Phoenix annexed the area in stages that were complete by 1987. The city now plans Ahwatukee Foothills as one of its urban villages, about 35.8 square miles, and a 2004 city profile counted 76,194 residents in 2000. Today roughly 85,000 people live there. For a pool owner, that history means a wide spread of ages: backyards first plastered in the 1970s and 1980s sit a few streets from pools built much later.

Pool maintenance Ahwatukee owners ask about, and the repairs that follow

Most calls are about routine upkeep: weekly pool cleaning and maintenance, water that will not stay clear, or a filter that clogs after a windy week. The next most common are fixes. A pump that will not prime or a leak at the equipment pad is pool repair territory. Hard water leaves a crusty white line on the tile, which is what pool tile cleaning is for. And in neighborhoods where the original plaster has seen several decades of sun, the question becomes whether to patch or to resurface the pool; the guide on repairing or resurfacing walks through that choice.

What City of Phoenix water does to an Ahwatukee pool

Ahwatukee taps run on City of Phoenix water. The city says about 58 percent of its supply comes from the Salt and Verde rivers, about 40 percent from the Colorado River through the Central Arizona Project canal, and about 2 percent from groundwater. All of it picks up minerals from the rock and soil it crosses on the way.

Hardness you can measure

The city's 2025 Water Quality Report lists total hardness of 172 to 302 milligrams per liter, or 10 to 17.6 grains per gallon. Hardness is mostly calcium and magnesium. It is not a health concern, and the city notes it is not regulated as one, but it is exactly what builds scale on pool tile and inside a heater.

Dissolved solids that stack up

The same report gives total dissolved solids, meaning everything dissolved in the water, at 464 to 716 parts per million. A pool loses water to evaporation but not its minerals, so each top off adds more. Over a few hot seasons the level climbs well above what came out of the hose.

What a careful technician watches

A good pool technician tests calcium hardness and stabilizer (cyanuric acid, which shields chlorine from the sun) along with chlorine and pH, and keeps the water balanced so it neither etches plaster nor drops scale. When readings get too high, a partial drain and refill is often the fix. Ask how they handle the drain water.

An Ahwatukee pool through the year

Summer sets the pace. The National Weather Service counted 70 days at or above 110 degrees at Phoenix Sky Harbor in 2024, a record, and 143 days at 100 or more. Warm water and high sun burn through free chlorine fast, so a pool that coasted on visits every other week in spring may need a weekly one by June. Water also leaves quickly in that heat, which is why the autofill or the hose gets a workout and why minerals build up.

Then come the storms. The weather service treats June 15 through September 30 as monsoon season. A wall of dust can roll across the Valley in the afternoon and leave a film on the water and a filter full of silt by evening, and the rain behind it washes debris off the deck and roof. If your yard backs onto open desert near the mountain, expect grit and plant litter in the water after a blow, and plan on a skimmer and filter check after every big storm.

Safe checks before you call

  • Baskets. Empty the skimmer and pump baskets. A basket packed with leaves or dust can starve the pump of water.
  • Filter gauge. Note the pressure reading and compare it to the normal clean reading. A big jump after a storm points to a dirty filter.
  • Water level. It should sit about halfway up the skimmer opening. Too low and the pump can pull air.
  • Timer. Make sure the clock or automation still shows the right time after a power blip from a storm.
  • Breaker. Reset a tripped GFCI or breaker once. If it trips again, leave it off and tell the company.

Safety first. Never mix pool chemicals with each other, and store them apart, dry, shaded and out of children's reach. A cloudy or green pool is a drowning hazard because no one can see the bottom, so keep children away until it is clear. If you smell gas near a pool heater, leave the area, do not touch the heater or any switch, and from a safe distance call Southwest Gas or 911.

Ahwatukee pool repair: older equipment, better questions

Because Ahwatukee started building in the 1970s and sold homes at a fast clip through the 1980s, a lot of its pools have been through several pumps and filters, a resurface or two, and some plumbing changes along the way. The house may be older than the equipment, or the reverse. Before you agree to replace a part, it helps to know what you have and what failed.

Questions to ask the pool company

  • What is your Arizona ROC license number? Repairs, equipment replacement and resurfacing generally call for a licensed contractor. Look the number up before work starts.
  • Is this a repair or a replacement? Ask what failed, whether a part can be swapped, and what a newer, more efficient pump would change.
  • Can you test the fill water as well as the pool? With hardness this high, a reading from the hose tells you where the pool started.
  • How will drained or backwashed water be handled? Ask where it will go on your property and how the company follows City of Phoenix rules.
  • What does the quote include? Get labor, parts and any follow-up visits listed in writing.
