Cave Creek: a desert foothills town with its own water

Cave Creek is an incorporated town in the desert foothills north of Phoenix, and it owns and operates its own water utility. The town bought the system in 2007. Since then, according to its annual water quality reports, it has relied on surface water from the CAP canal, which carries Colorado River water across the desert. A 16 inch raw water pipeline runs more than 12 miles north from the canal to the town's treatment plant, and the town's 2023 report notes that no wells are currently part of its drinking water supply.

The town reports on two systems: the Cave Creek Water System and the Desert Hills Water System. They are reported separately, so readings can differ. If you are not sure which serves your home, your water bill or the town's Utilities Department can tell you. The town also runs a wastewater system. Whether your home is on town sewer or a septic system matters if the pool ever has to be drained, so find out before that day comes.

Hard CAP water and an open desert yard

The town's 2018 water quality report listed hardness of 12 to 15 grains per gallon for the Cave Creek system, with sodium at 91 parts per million. Grains per gallon is the unit most water softener makers use. The U.S. Geological Survey calls water above 180 milligrams per liter very hard, which is about 10.5 grains per gallon. Pool water starts at that level and climbs as the sun evaporates water and refills add more minerals. A pool company that tests calcium hardness along with chlorine and pH can keep that from turning into scale on the tile and inside the equipment.

The yard matters too. A pool open to the desert on one or more sides catches what the wind carries off the lot: dust, leaves, seed pods and bits of dry plant. That shows up as full skimmer baskets, a filter that loads up faster than you expect, and fine silt on the pool floor. During the monsoon season, which the National Weather Service sets as June 15 to September 30, a single dust storm can leave a pool cloudy and the filter pressure high. The monsoon pool care guide covers what to do after one.

Safe checks on a desert lot

  • Clear the baskets. Empty the skimmer basket, and the pump basket with the pump off, after any windy day.
  • Look around the equipment pad. Clear leaves and debris away from the pump and filter so they can cool, and note any drips.
  • Read the filter gauge. Write down the pressure. A jump after a storm usually means the filter needs cleaning.
  • Reset once, then stop. If a GFCI or breaker for the pool has tripped, reset it one time. If it trips again, leave it off and call.

Safety and licensing for Cave Creek repairs

Repair work is where checking the company counts most. Arizona's Registrar of Contractors licenses contractors, and pool equipment, repair and resurfacing work generally calls for a licensed contractor. Ask for the license number and look it up yourself before anyone starts.

Safety first. If you smell gas near a pool heater, leave the area, do not use switches or the heater, and from a safe distance call your gas utility's emergency line or 911. Never mix pool chemicals with each other, keep them stored apart and out of children's reach, and keep children away from a pool that is unfenced, uncovered or too cloudy to see the bottom.

Questions to ask a Cave Creek pool company

  • What is your ROC license number, and what classification is it? Then check it on the ROC search.
  • Is the repair price written down before work starts? Ask for parts and labor separately.
  • How will you handle drained or backwashed water on my lot? Ask which town or HOA rule they follow.
  • Do you test calcium hardness? With very hard fill water, it belongs on every visit's list.
