Where Anthem's water comes from

EPCOR's report for the Anthem system names its sources as Colorado River water carried by the Central Arizona Project (CAP) and groundwater from the Salt River Valley basin. The CAP water reaches Anthem by pipeline from the CAP canal and is treated at the Anthem Water Treatment Plant, which the utility describes as a microfiltration plant. The report also notes that the water may be a mix of Colorado River water and Lake Pleasant water, and Lake Pleasant takes in water from the Agua Fria River.

Microfiltration is a very fine filter. It strains out particles and microbes, but it does not take out dissolved calcium and magnesium, so the treated water stays hard. And because the blend can shift between sources, the water you fill with in one season may not test the same as the water you fill with in another.

For scale, 18 grains per gallon works out to roughly 300 milligrams per liter of hardness. Total dissolved solids (TDS) is everything dissolved in the water, minerals and salts together. The U.S. EPA's secondary, non-enforceable guideline for TDS in drinking water is 500 milligrams per liter, so Anthem's fill water starts above that mark before a single gallon evaporates from the pool.

What very hard fill water does to a pool

Evaporation takes water and leaves minerals behind. Each top off with 18 grain water adds more. These are the places it shows up first.

The waterline tile

Calcium deposits form a white or gray band where the water meets the tile. Caught early it comes off with care. Left for years it hardens, and removing it becomes a job for a professional.

Heaters and salt cells

Heat pulls calcium out of the water, so scale collects inside heater exchangers and on salt cell plates. A scaled heater runs hot and heats poorly, and a scaled cell makes less chlorine.

Plaster and pebble finishes

Water that is out of balance either leaves scale on the surface or slowly etches it. A careful technician tests calcium, pH, alkalinity and stabilizer together and adjusts toward balance.

Looking after an Anthem pool between visits

You do not need to be a chemist. A few safe habits help a pool company keep hard water in check:

  • Top off before it drops far. Keep the water about halfway up the skimmer opening so the pump does not draw air.
  • Watch the waterline. Mention new white crust right away. Fresh deposits are easier to remove than old ones.
  • Note the filter gauge. A pressure reading that keeps climbing means the filter is loading up with dust and scale.
  • Test with a home kit. Strips or a drop kit give you chlorine and pH readings to share. Leave adjustments with strong chemicals to the pool company.
  • Check the heater area. If you smell gas near a gas pool heater, leave the area, do not touch switches or the heater, and from a safe distance call your gas utility's emergency line or 911.

Store chemicals safely. Never mix pool chemicals with each other. Keep them apart, dry, shaded and out of children's reach, follow the label, and leave acid work to a professional. And never leave a child alone near the pool, even for a moment.

Questions to ask an Anthem pool company

  • Do you test calcium hardness and TDS, and how often? In Anthem water these matter as much as chlorine.
  • What is your Arizona ROC license number? Ask before repairs, equipment, resurfacing or leak work, then check it on the ROC search.
  • How do you handle scale on the tile and in the heater? Ask what method they use and what it costs before work starts.
  • If you drain the pool, where does the water go? Ask them to name the county or HOA rule they follow.
