San Tan Valley: from county subdivisions to a town of its own

San Tan Valley sits in Pinal County, next door to Queen Creek. It filled with subdivisions without ever being a city, so for most of its life county government handled the local work a town hall would. Supporters tried for incorporation more than once. A 2022 change in state law limited which nearby cities could block a new town; under it, only Gilbert and Mesa had to approve. Gilbert said yes in August 2024 and Mesa a few weeks later.

Petitions went in during April 2025, and county elections staff accepted 10,641 signatures against the 6,107 needed. On August 5, 2025, voters decided Proposition 495, and early returns showed about 66 percent in favor out of 12,343 ballots counted. The incorporation date was set for July 1, 2026, making San Tan Valley Arizona's 92nd municipality and, with more than 100,000 residents, the largest incorporation in state history.

Groundwater and a San Tan Valley pool

EPCOR's 2025 report for its San Tan system says the water comes only from wells. That makes your provider's report, not a Phoenix figure, the one to read.

Wells, not rivers

Many Valley cities blend river water from the Salt, the Verde or the Colorado. EPCOR's San Tan system draws on groundwater alone, inside the Phoenix Active Management Area, a region where Arizona law limits and tracks groundwater pumping.

Minerals that stay behind

Whatever hardness and dissolved solids are in the tap water stay in the pool when water evaporates. Look up those lines in your provider's annual report, then ask the pool company how quickly they are building in your pool.

Balance before scale

A careful technician tracks calcium hardness, pH, alkalinity and stabilizer together, so the water does not leave a crust on the tile or eat into the finish. When levels get too high, a partial drain and refill is the usual reset.

Heat, dust storms and a full skimmer

In the Phoenix area's summer, the sun and warm water strip chlorine out of a pool quickly, and evaporation pulls the level down by the week. Then monsoon season, which the National Weather Service counts from June 15 to September 30, brings wind and dust. A storm can leave a gray film on the water, a skimmer full of leaves and a filter that needs cleaning well ahead of schedule. A pool that sits in that state for a few days can start to cloud or turn green.

Five checks that are safe for any homeowner

  • Skimmer and pump baskets: empty them after any windy day.
  • Filter gauge: compare today's reading with the clean reading. A big rise means the filter is loaded.
  • Water level: keep it near the middle of the skimmer opening so the pump does not run dry.
  • Timer or automation: confirm the schedule after the power flickers.
  • Equipment pad: look for drips, wet ground or a tripped GFCI. Reset a trip once, and if it trips again leave it off.

Safety comes first. A green pool hides the bottom, so it is a drowning hazard: keep children out of the yard until it clears. Never combine pool chemicals; store each one separately, dry, shaded and out of reach. If you smell gas around a pool heater, move away, leave switches and the heater alone, and call the gas utility on your bill or 911 from a safe spot.

Hiring for pool work in a brand-new town

A new town takes time to set up its own departments and codes. If a job could need a permit, such as replacing a heater or changing plumbing, ask the pool company who issues it for your address today. Ask too where drained or backwashed water will go, and whether your homeowners association, if you have one, has rules about it.
