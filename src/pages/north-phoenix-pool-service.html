North Phoenix: five urban villages, one set of city rules

Phoenix plans itself as a set of urban villages. The northern ones are North Mountain, Paradise Valley Village, Deer Valley, Desert View and North Gateway. The city's Desert View map, for example, starts at the Central Arizona Project canal and carries on north to the Carefree Highway. So North Phoenix covers everything from streets that grew up around the mountain preserves to subdivisions in open desert.

That spread matters for a pool. A house near the mountain preserve may have a pool decades old, with original plumbing and plaster that has seen many summers. A house in the far north may have a young pool with variable speed equipment and automation. ZIP codes such as 85020, 85028, 85053 and 85085 all count as North Phoenix, and all of them are still the City of Phoenix, so the same water, sewer and fence rules apply across the whole area.

City of Phoenix water in a North Phoenix pool

The City of Phoenix publishes a water quality report each year. Its 2025 report lists total hardness of 172 to 302 milligrams per liter across the system, or 10 to 17.6 grains per gallon. Hardness means dissolved calcium and magnesium. The city explains that these minerals come from the soils the source water runs through on the way to its treatment plants, and that hardness is a matter of taste and scale, not a health standard.

A pool concentrates those minerals. Sun and dry air pull pure water off the surface, the minerals stay behind, and each top off adds more. Over a few summers, calcium hardness in the pool can climb well past what came out of the tap. That shows up as a white or gray crust at the waterline, rough spots on plaster, and scale inside a heater or a salt cell. A careful pool technician tests calcium hardness, pH, total alkalinity and stabilizer (cyanuric acid, which shields chlorine from sunlight) and reads them together, because the balance between them decides whether the water leaves scale behind or eats at the surface.

Safe checks before you call

  • Baskets. Empty the skimmer basket and, with the pump off, the pump basket. Desert leaves and palo verde litter fill them fast.
  • Filter pressure. Note the gauge reading. A reading well above normal for your pool usually means the filter is due for cleaning.
  • Water level. Water should sit about halfway up the skimmer opening. Too low and the pump can draw air.
  • Timer. Check the clock or automation schedule, which can reset after a summer power outage.
  • Breaker. A tripped GFCI or breaker gets a single reset. A second trip means hands off until a professional looks.

Chemical safety. Never mix pool chemicals with each other. Store them apart, dry, shaded and out of children's reach, follow the label, and leave acid work to a professional. A green or cloudy pool hides the bottom, which makes it a drowning hazard, so keep children well away until it is clear.

Draining and backwashing under Phoenix rules

The City of Phoenix publishes plain rules for where pool water can go. Ask any company that drains or backwashes your pool to follow them.

Not the street or alley

The city says not to drain pool water into the street, an alley or any city right of way, and city code bars pool water from the storm drains.

Slowly into the sewer cleanout

A full drain goes into the home's sanitary sewer cleanout, usually a capped pipe near the house. The city sets 12 gallons per minute as the most it recommends, says the safe rate may be lower, and advises watching shower and tub drains for backups.

Backwash on the landscape

The city lists watering your own landscaping as an acceptable use for backwash or drained pool water. It also says dirty backwash and saltwater pool water must not be discharged off your property.

The city pool barrier code in North Phoenix

Phoenix adopted the 2024 International Swimming Pool and Spa Code with local amendments, effective August 1, 2025. Its barrier handout lays out the basics: a barrier at least 60 inches (five feet) tall measured from the outside, no opening a 4 inch ball can pass through, and gates that close and latch by themselves, swing away from the pool and carry the latch at least 54 inches above the ground. When part of an existing enclosure is changed, the city says the new work must meet the current barrier standard.

This matters when a pool company recommends replacing equipment near a gate, or when an older yard has a gap where a wall meets the house. The line does not inspect fences, but it helps to know the standard. Whatever the fence, never leave a child alone near water, and keep children away from a pool that is unfenced or uncovered.

Questions to ask a North Phoenix pool company

  • What is your Arizona ROC license number? Ask before any repair, equipment, resurfacing or leak work, then look it up yourself.
  • How will you handle drained or backwashed water? The answer should be the sewer cleanout or your landscaping, never the street.
  • Which readings do you test each visit? In hard city water, calcium hardness belongs on the list along with chlorine and pH.
  • What is in the weekly price, and what is extra? Get the answer in writing.
