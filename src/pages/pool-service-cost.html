How much does pool service cost in Phoenix? There is no single number, and anyone who quotes one over the phone without seeing the pool is guessing. The price depends on the size of the pool, how often it is visited, who supplies the chemicals, how much falls into the water and the age of the equipment. This guide pulls together published 2026 ranges for recurring service, one-time cleanups and common repairs, explains what moves a Phoenix price up or down, and shows how to compare quotes fairly.

A word on where these numbers come from. Phoenix Pool Cleaning Pro is a free phone line, not a pool company, and it never prices a job. A call is handed to an independent, locally owned pool service company in the Valley, and that company inspects the pool and sets its own price. Each dollar figure on this page is credited to the cost guide that published it, with links at the end. Treat them as background reading: actual quotes for Phoenix pools vary.

What moves a Phoenix pool service price up or down

Two pools on the same street can get very different quotes. These are the things a company looks at before it names a price.

Hard water is a real factor here. The City of Phoenix's 2025 water quality report lists tap water hardness of 172 to 302 parts per million. As pools lose water to evaporation and are topped off, those minerals build up, which is one reason local companies add scale treatment.

Monthly pool service cost vs one-time cleanups

Recurring service spreads the work out. A company that sees the pool every week knows its habits, catches small changes early and keeps chemistry steady, so each visit is a known amount of work. That predictability is part of what you pay for.

One-time cleanups cost more per visit because the company is starting from an unknown condition. HomeGuide's 2026 guide puts a one-time cleaning at $200 to $400, and a green pool's first cleanup at $300 to $500 before ongoing service. A pool left green for weeks may need several visits, filter cleaning and plenty of chemicals before it is clear again.

If the pool is green now, start with the green pool cleanup page, then compare monthly plans once the water is clear.

How to compare pool service quotes

The quote with the smallest number is not always the smallest bill over a year. Put quotes side by side on the same terms:

  • Same billing period. Convert everything to a monthly or yearly figure. Some quotes are per visit, some per month.
  • Same tasks. Does each visit include brushing, vacuuming and both baskets, or only a chemical check?
  • Chemicals. Which are in the price, which are extra and how will you see what was used?
  • Filter cleaning. How often, and is it included or billed separately?
  • Storms and extras. What happens after a dust storm, and what does an extra visit cost?
  • Repairs. Are repairs always quoted in writing before work starts? Does the company hold an Arizona ROC license for repair work?
  • Terms. How much notice to cancel, and is there a contract length?

For the questions that matter when you pick a company, see the guide to choosing a pool company. For what a service plan should include, see pool service in Phoenix.

How much is pool maintenance in Arizona over a year?

The service bill is only part of owning a pool here. HomeGuide puts total yearly upkeep, including service, chemicals, electricity, water and repairs, at about $3,000 to $6,000. Water is part of it: the Arizona Department of Water Resources estimates a standard uncovered pool loses four to six feet of water a year to evaporation, and says a pool cover can save 90 to 95 percent of that loss. Budgeting for the full picture makes the monthly service price easier to judge.
