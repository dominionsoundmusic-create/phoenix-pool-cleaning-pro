How a pool pump works, part by part

Think of the pump in two halves. The motor is the electric half. The wet end, the part that touches pool water, is the plastic housing bolted to the front of it. The motor spins a shaft, and the shaft spins an impeller inside the housing. The spinning impeller throws water outward, which pulls more water in from the pool and pushes it on to the filter.

Every part on the list below can cause a different symptom, which is why a pump that "does not work" can mean five different repairs.

{{ img('pool/repair-wide.jpg', 'Two hands turning a wrench on an old cast pool pump in a workshop', 1200, 500) }}
  • MotorThe electric drive. Older single-speed motors run at one fixed speed. Variable-speed motors have built-in electronics that let them run slower for most of the day.
  • ImpellerThe spinning vaned disc inside the housing that moves the water. Small debris, like a pebble or a seed pod, can lodge in its vanes and choke the flow.
  • DiffuserA shroud around the impeller that guides water toward the outlet.
  • Shaft sealA two-piece mechanical seal where the motor shaft passes into the wet end. It keeps water out of the motor. When it wears, water drips under the pump.
  • Strainer basketA basket in the front pot that catches leaves and debris before they reach the impeller.
  • Lid and lid O-ringThe clear or solid lid over the basket, sealed by a rubber ring. A dry, cracked or pinched O-ring lets air in, and the pump loses prime.

Why Phoenix pumps work so hard

Valley pools run all year, and the pump sits outside through the whole desert summer. The National Weather Service office in Phoenix recorded an average high of about 112 degrees for July 2024, with peaks of 118 degrees, and ranked it the second warmest July on record by average temperature. Most equipment pads are on the sunny side of a block wall, often in a narrow side yard where hot air does not move. A motor cools itself with the air around it, so a pad stacked with leaves, boxes or a tight shade cover traps heat and makes thermal shutoffs more likely.

Debris is the other strain. Monsoon storms push dust, leaves and palm fronds into pools, and a full pump basket or a clogged impeller forces the motor to work against a blockage. Hard water adds scale inside heaters and salt cells downstream, which raises resistance in the whole system. None of this means a pump will fail on a schedule, but it explains why basket checks and clear airflow matter more here.

Rebuild the pump or replace it?

"Can a pool pump be repaired?" usually gets a yes. The question is which parts, and whether fixing them makes sense for the age of the pump.

Usually worth repairing

  • A cracked or flattened lid O-ring, or a cracked lid
  • A broken strainer basket
  • A leaking shaft seal caught before water ruins the bearings
  • A clogged or chipped impeller, or a worn diffuser
  • A failed start component on a single-speed motor
  • A worn motor on a wet end that is still in good shape

Often time to replace

  • A cracked housing or pot
  • Parts no longer made for that model
  • A burned-out motor and a worn wet end at the same time
  • A pump the wrong size for the pool and filter
  • Repeated failures from the same old unit
  • An owner who wants the energy savings of a variable-speed pump

Ask the pool company for both numbers when the choice is close: the repair now, and a replacement. Then weigh them against how long the rest of the pump is likely to last. Federal efficiency rules for pool pumps and motors

Two U.S. Department of Energy rules matter when a Phoenix pump is replaced. They apply to equipment made after the compliance dates. They do not make anyone remove a pump that still works.

U.S. Department of Energy pool pump and pool pump motor efficiency dates
DateWhat it coversWhat it means for you
July 19, 2021Dedicated-purpose pool pumps, the whole pumpMost new pool pump types had to meet minimum efficiency levels, measured as a weighted energy factor.
September 29, 2025Pool pump motors from 1.15 to 5 total horsepowerMotors made on or after this date must have variable-speed control. This rule also reaches motors sold by themselves, the kind used when an old pump gets a new motor.
September 28, 2027Smaller motors from 0.5 to under 1.15 total horsepowerThe same variable-speed requirement for the smaller size class. In an April 2026 enforcement policy, the Department said it would delay enforcement for these smaller motors made before March 26, 2029. The standard itself did not change.

A variable-speed pump can run slowly for long stretches, which moves the same water with much less electricity, and speed up for vacuuming or a spa. It also needs to be set up well: the speeds and hours should suit the pool, the filter and any heater or salt cell that needs a minimum flow. Ask the technician to show you the schedule they program.

Electric bills, APS and SRP

Most Valley homes buy power from Arizona Public Service (APS) or Salt River Project (SRP), depending on the address. APS's energy-saving tips suggest running a pool pump during off-peak hours if you are on a time-of-use plan, and a variable-speed pump makes long, slow off-peak runs practical. Utility rebates come and go. SRP says its residential pool pump rebate is no longer offered, and this page did not find a current residential pool pump rebate from APS, so ask your utility before you count on one.

What a careful pool technician does on a pump call

A good pump diagnosis starts with the water side, because many "pump problems" are really suction problems. Expect the technician to check the water level and baskets, look at the lid and O-ring, watch the basket for air while the pump runs, and check the filter pressure. If the motor will not run or trips power, they test it with the right instruments instead of guessing, and check for water that has reached the motor through a failed seal. They should also look at where the pump sits: shade, airflow and clearance from walls and debris.
