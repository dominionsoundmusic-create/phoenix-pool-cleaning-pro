This is a young city, incorporated in 2003, built largely in the years since, with its own water utilities and its own county. Below is what that means for a backyard pool here, and how a single free call reaches a pool company that works in Maricopa.

Maricopa: a Pinal County city, not Maricopa County

The name causes real confusion. The City of Maricopa sits in Pinal County, south of Phoenix and outside Maricopa County altogether. When you look up a county rule, a county health contact or a building question for a Maricopa address, you want Pinal County or the City of Maricopa, not the Maricopa County offices that serve Phoenix, Mesa and the rest of the core Valley.

Before 2003 Maricopa was an unincorporated community; the 2000 census counted 1,040 people. The city incorporated on October 15, 2003, as Arizona's 88th municipality. A special census in December 2005 already found 15,934 residents, the 2020 census counted 58,125, and the city's own estimate is now about 73,300, roughly a quarter more than in 2020. Growth at that pace means most homes and pools in town are fairly young.

Water in Maricopa: read your own provider's report

Maricopa homes are not on City of Phoenix water, so the Phoenix hardness figures you see online do not describe your tap. Water here comes from local providers, and each one publishes its own annual Consumer Confidence Report.

Find your provider

Global Water's Santa Cruz Water Company publishes a report for its Maricopa system, listed under state system number AZ04-11-131. If a different company bills you, look for its report instead.

Look for two lines

Hardness (often in milligrams per liter as calcium carbonate; divide by about 17.1 for grains per gallon) and total dissolved solids. Those two numbers are what the pool inherits every time it is topped off.

Have the fill water tested

A report gives ranges across a whole system. A pool technician testing the hose water and the pool side by side shows how fast minerals are building in your own water.

Keeping a young Maricopa pool in good shape

Newer pools fail in their own ways. Fresh plaster or pebble finishes need careful water balance early on, because water that is too aggressive can etch a new surface and water that is too scale-forming can leave a rough, cloudy film. If you bought the house with a pool already in, ask the company to check stabilizer (cyanuric acid, which protects chlorine from sunlight) as well as chlorine and pH. Stabilized chlorine tablets add it with every dose, and in a pool that never gets partially drained the level can climb high enough to make chlorine sluggish.

Weather adds the rest. The National Weather Service's monsoon season runs June 15 through September 30, and its storms can push dust, tumbleweeds and yard debris over the wall in minutes. A filter that handled spring with ease can load up after one afternoon. Plan a basket and filter check after each storm, and expect the pump to need more run time in the heat.

Safe things to check yourself

  • Pump and skimmer baskets full of debris after wind.
  • Filter pressure compared with its usual clean reading.
  • Water level at about the middle of the skimmer opening.
  • Automation or timer settings after a power outage.
  • Drips or puddles around the equipment pad. Note where, and tell the company.

Safety. Keep pool chemicals in their original containers, never combine them, and store them apart in a dry, shady place children cannot reach. Keep kids away from a pool you cannot see the bottom of. If you smell gas near a heater, get away from it, do not use switches, and call the gas utility on your bill (Southwest Gas serves most of the Phoenix area) or 911 once you are clear.

Equipment from the building boom

Because so much of Maricopa went up in a short span, many homes on a street share roughly the same pool equipment age, and the first round of pump motors, filter grids and heater parts can come due around the same years. If a neighbor just replaced a pump, yours may not be far behind. That is a reason to ask the pool company to look at the whole pad, not just the part that quit, and to explain which items are worth repairing and which are near the end.
