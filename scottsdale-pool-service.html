Scottsdale's Water Is Among the Hardest in the Valley

Scottsdale draws from a mix of Salt River Project surface water, Central Arizona Project canal water, and local groundwater wells. The blend varies by neighborhood and season, but the result is consistently hard — calcium hardness readings above 400 ppm are routine, and some North Scottsdale neighborhoods on well-heavy supply exceed 600. For comparison, pool equipment manufacturers recommend staying below 200.

That hardness hits pool equipment relentlessly. Salt cells scale in half their rated life. Heater heat exchangers crust with calcium that insulates the metal and forces the burner to run hotter. Waterline tile develops a white crust within 12 to 18 months. The only defense is weekly chemistry monitoring and regular equipment inspection — which is what good weekly service provides.

What Every Weekly Visit Covers

The technician follows the same checklist every week. Skim the surface, empty baskets, brush walls and tile, vacuum the floor. Test chlorine, pH, alkalinity, cyanuric acid, and calcium hardness — adjust what needs adjusting. Check the filter pressure gauge, inspect the pump, and look at every piece of visible equipment. If something is off, you hear about it the same day.

Chemicals are included. Chlorine, acid, stabilizer, and anything else the pool needs on a routine basis are part of the monthly price. One number on your bill, nothing extra.

Scottsdale Neighborhoods

Old Town and South Scottsdale

Older homes with established pools — many built in the 1970s and 1980s with plaster surfaces and aging equipment. These pools benefit most from consistent service because the equipment is closer to end-of-life and the plaster is more porous, which means calcium stains faster.

North Scottsdale

DC Ranch, Grayhawk, McDowell Mountain Ranch, Troon, Pinnacle Peak, and Desert Ridge. Newer homes, larger pools, and often more complex setups — spas, water features, automation systems, salt chlorine generators. The water in North Scottsdale tends to be harder than the south because of heavier reliance on groundwater wells.

Central Scottsdale

Gainey Ranch, McCormick Ranch, Kierland, and the Scottsdale Road corridor. A mix of single-family homes, townhomes with community pools, and vacation rentals. A good pool service services private residential pools — not HOA or commercial pools.

Vacation Rental Pools

Scottsdale has one of the highest concentrations of short-term rentals in the Valley, and many of them have pools. A good pool service services several vacation rental and Airbnb pools and coordinates around guest schedules — service before check-in so the pool is perfect when guests arrive, and a post-checkout check to catch anything that needs attention before the next booking. If a guest reports a pool issue, a good pool service responds the same day.

Repairs

When something breaks, a good pool service diagnoses and fixes it on site. Pumps, motors, filters, heaters, salt cells, lights, and plumbing leaks. The company that already cleans your pool is often the quickest call, because its technician already knows your equipment. Repairs are quoted separately and you see the price before any work starts.
