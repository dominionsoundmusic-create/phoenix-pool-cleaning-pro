Frequently Asked Questions

Answers to the most common questions about pool cleaning, repair, and maintenance in Phoenix.

Weekly Service & Pricing

What is included in weekly pool service?

Every visit includes skimming, brushing walls and tile, vacuuming, emptying baskets, testing and adjusting water chemistry (chlorine, pH, alkalinity, cyanuric acid, calcium hardness), checking filter pressure, and inspecting equipment. All routine chemicals are included in the monthly price.

How often should a Phoenix pool be cleaned?

Weekly. Phoenix pools run year round in the hardest water in Arizona. Chemistry drifts faster, equipment scales faster, and chlorine burns off faster in summer heat than in any mild climate. Weekly is the minimum that keeps a pool consistently swimmable.

Are chemicals included or extra?

Included. Chlorine, acid, stabilizer, and any routine chemicals are part of the monthly price. One bill, no line items. The only exception is a large one-time treatment like an algae cleanout, quoted separately.

How much does weekly pool service cost?

Monthly pricing depends on pool size, spa, and sanitization type. Call with your address and pool size for a flat monthly quote. Chemicals included, no per-visit charges, no seasonal surcharges.

Do you service salt water pools?

Yes. Salt pools still need weekly brushing, chemistry testing, and equipment checks. We inspect the salt cell every visit for calcium scale. In Phoenix's hard water, cells scale faster than the manufacturer's timeline — regular inspection extends cell life.

Phoenix Water & Climate

Why is Phoenix pool water so hard?

Phoenix draws water from the Colorado River via the Central Arizona Project and from Salt River Project reservoirs and wells. Both sources carry high mineral loads from traveling through limestone formations. Tap water in the Valley runs 300 to 900 ppm calcium hardness — two to four times what equipment manufacturers recommend.

What happens during monsoon season?

Monsoon storms dump dust and debris into pools and can turn water green within 48 hours. During June through September we increase chemical dosing and cleanup. If a storm hits between visits, call and we will come out at no extra charge on most plans.

How do I know if my pool is leaking or just evaporating?

Set a bucket on a pool step and mark both levels. Wait 24 hours. If the pool dropped more than the bucket, you have a leak. Phoenix pools can lose a quarter inch per day to evaporation in summer.

Equipment Repair

Should I repair my pool pump or replace it?

If the motor is the only problem, a motor swap costs roughly half of a full replacement. If the repair exceeds half the cost of a new variable-speed pump, replacement makes more sense — the energy savings pay back the difference within 12 to 18 months in Phoenix's year-round season.

How much does a pump replacement cost?

Motor-only swap: $250–$500. Full single-speed replacement: $450–$800. Variable-speed replacement: $1,200–$1,800 installed, but cuts energy costs 60–80%.

Why does equipment fail faster in Phoenix?

Three reasons: the water is two to four times harder than manufacturers spec for, the ambient heat pushes motor temperatures higher, and pools run twelve months instead of seven or eight. A pump rated for eight years may last five here.

Tile Cleaning

How often should pool tile be cleaned in Phoenix?

Every one to two years. Phoenix's 300–900 ppm water builds visible calcium scale on waterline tile faster than almost anywhere else.

Does tile cleaning require draining the pool?

Not for the blasting. We lower water six to eight inches below the tile line, blast, then refill. A partial drain-and-refill may be recommended afterward to reduce calcium hardness.
