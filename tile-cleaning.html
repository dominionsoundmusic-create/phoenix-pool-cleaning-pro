Why Phoenix Tile Scales Faster Than Anywhere Else

Phoenix tap water runs 300 to 900 parts per million calcium hardness. At the waterline, where evaporation is highest, that calcium deposits as a white or gray crust on the tile surface. In softer-water cities this takes three to five years. In Phoenix it can happen in 12 to 18 months — sometimes faster on pools with raised walls, water features, or spillover spas where water is constantly evaporating from exposed surfaces.

The scale is not just cosmetic. Once it bonds to the tile, it hardens into a calcium carbonate crust that household chemicals and scrub pads cannot remove. Muriatic acid applied by hand works on light buildup but damages grout and the tile surface if used repeatedly. The only method that removes heavy scale without damaging the tile is media blasting with the right media.

How We Clean It

We lower the water level six to eight inches below the tile line, then blast the scale off with a compressed-air gun loaded with calcium-ite or kieserite media. This media is softer than glass bead — it dissolves on impact, which means it breaks the calcium bond without scratching the tile glaze or tearing out grout. The tile comes out looking like it did when the pool was built.

Glass bead blasting is the other method you will see advertised, and it works — but it is abrasive enough to strip the glaze off ceramic and porcelain tile. Once the glaze is gone, the tile surface is rough, and rough surfaces attract calcium faster. That means more frequent cleanings and eventually tile replacement. We do not use glass bead for that reason.

After the blast

We refill the pool to its normal level, rebalance the chemistry, and clean up the blasting debris from the deck. The pool is swimmable the same day. If your calcium hardness is extremely high — above 500 ppm — we may recommend a partial drain-and-refill to bring the number down and slow the next round of buildup.

How Often

Every one to two years for most Phoenix pools. Pools with weekly service tend to go longer because the chemistry is consistently managed and the saturation index is kept in range. Pools without regular service — or pools on well water in the far West Valley and North Scottsdale — may need cleaning annually. If you can see a white or gray line forming at the water level, it is time.

Tile Cleaning Questions

How often should pool tile be cleaned in Phoenix?

Every one to two years for most Phoenix pools. The Valley's 300–900 ppm calcium water builds visible scale on the waterline faster than almost anywhere else. If you see a white or gray crust forming at the tile line, it is time regardless of the calendar.

Does tile cleaning require draining the pool?

Not for the blasting itself. We lower the water six to eight inches below the tile line, blast, then refill. A partial drain-and-refill afterward is sometimes recommended to bring calcium hardness down.

What blasting media do you use?

We use a calcium-ite or kieserite media that dissolves on impact. It removes calcium scale without scratching the tile glaze or damaging grout — unlike glass bead, which is abrasive enough to strip the finish.

How much does pool tile cleaning cost in Phoenix?

Pricing depends on pool size, the linear footage of tile, and the severity of the scale. Most residential pools fall in the $350 to $600 range. Call with your pool size for an exact quote.

Can I swim the same day after tile cleaning?

Yes. The media dissolves and does not leave residue. We rebalance the chemistry after blasting and the pool is swimmable the same day.
