Pools at the edge of the Valley

Apache Junction sits on the far eastern edge of the metro, right under the Superstitions, and its pools deal with a slightly different set of problems from those in central Phoenix. There is more open desert around town, so there is more dust. A large share of homes belong to winter residents, so a lot of pools go unwatched from April to October, exactly the months when the heat is hardest on them. And the water is as hard as anywhere in the East Valley.

If you leave for the summer

This is the most common question from Apache Junction pool owners. A pool left alone in 110-degree heat can go from clear to green in a week once the chlorine is gone, and a pump that stops or a timer that resets can do the damage before anyone notices. Weekly service while you are away covers the chemistry, keeps baskets and filters clear, and gives someone a reason to look at the equipment every week. Ask the company you are connected with to send a short note or photo after each visit, so you can see the pool from wherever you spend the summer.

When you come back in the fall, it is also the right time to have the filter cleaned and the equipment looked over before you start using the pool again.

Monsoon dust and debris

From July into September, monsoon storms push walls of dust across the open desert east of the Valley. After one blows through, a pool can be full of silt, leaves and grit, the skimmer and pump baskets packed, and the chlorine badly depleted. Empty the baskets and keep the pump running, then have the water tested and the pool vacuumed within a day or two. Leaving it longer is how a dust storm turns into an algae problem.

Hard water and scale

Apache Junction's water carries a lot of dissolved calcium. Every time water evaporates, and in the desert it evaporates fast, the calcium stays behind and concentrates. It shows up as a white crust at the waterline, and less visibly as scale inside heaters and on salt cell plates. Keeping the water balanced every week slows it down. Once scale is on the tile, it takes a proper tile cleaning to remove it.

Repairs

Apache Junction pool questions

Can someone look after my pool while I'm gone for the summer?

Yes. Many Apache Junction pools belong to winter residents who leave from spring to fall. Weekly service while you are away keeps the water from turning green in the heat, keeps the pump and filter running, and catches equipment problems before you come back to a swamp. Ask for a short report or photo after visits.

How hard is the water in Apache Junction?

Hard. Like most of the East Valley, Apache Junction's water carries a lot of calcium, which leaves white scale on tile and inside heaters and salt cells. Regular testing and balancing, plus periodic tile cleaning, keeps it under control.

What happens to my pool in a monsoon dust storm?

Dust and debris blow in by the bucketful, clog skimmer and pump baskets, and cloud the water. Chlorine gets used up fast. After a storm, empty the baskets, run the pump, and have the chemistry checked within a day or two.

Who fixes pumps and equipment?

The pool company you are put through to. In Arizona, repair jobs over about $1,000 in labor and materials generally require a contractor licensed by the Arizona Registrar of Contractors, which you can check at roc.az.gov.
