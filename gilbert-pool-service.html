Gilbert's Growth Means More Pools Than Ever

Gilbert has grown from a farming community to one of the largest towns in Arizona, and the growth has filled the southeast Valley with master-planned neighborhoods where nearly every home has a pool. Power Ranch, Seville, Spectrum, and Gilbert Gateway all went up in the last 15 to 20 years, and the pools that came with them are now hitting the age where equipment starts to need attention — pump motors wearing out, heaters scaling, salt cells losing output.

The water here does not help. Gilbert draws from both Salt River Project surface water and the town's own groundwater wells. Calcium hardness typically runs 300 to 500 ppm, with the southern neighborhoods closer to Queen Creek running harder because of heavier well use. That calcium shortens equipment life, crusts the tile line, and shifts chemistry faster than a homeowner checking once a month can keep up with.

What Every Weekly Visit Covers

The technician follows the same checklist every week. Skim the surface, empty baskets, brush walls and tile, vacuum the floor. Test chlorine, pH, alkalinity, cyanuric acid, and calcium hardness — adjust what needs adjusting. Check the filter pressure, inspect the pump, and look at every piece of visible equipment. If something is off, you hear about it the same day.

Chemicals are included. One flat number every month, no line items, no seasonal surcharges.

Gilbert Neighborhoods We Serve

Agritopia and Heritage District

The heart of Gilbert — a mix of historic properties and newer infill. Pools here range from original 1990s builds with aging plaster and single-speed pumps to recent renovations with salt systems and automation. We service both and know what to look for at each age.

Power Ranch and Seville

Large master-planned communities in southeast Gilbert with thousands of pools. Most homes were built between 2005 and 2015, which means the original equipment — pumps, heaters, salt cells — is now 10 to 20 years old. This is the age range where proactive maintenance saves the most money, because a motor swap at year 12 costs a fraction of a full pump replacement at year 15.

Val Vista and Spectrum

Newer construction along the Val Vista corridor with modern pool setups — variable-speed pumps, LED lighting, smart automation. These pools are easier to maintain but still run in the same hard water. The technology just means the weekly inspection catches different things — a firmware update on the automation controller, a pH sensor that has drifted, a variable-speed program that is running higher than it needs to.
