The Hardest Water in Arizona

Phoenix tap water runs between 300 and 900 parts per million calcium hardness depending on where in the Valley you are. Some neighborhoods pull groundwater that exceeds 1,000 ppm. For comparison, pool equipment manufacturers recommend keeping calcium below 200. Every pool in Phoenix is over that limit the day it fills, and the number only climbs as the sun evaporates water and leaves the minerals behind.

That calcium deposits on everything — heater cores, salt cell plates, filter grids, pump internals, and the waterline tile. A heater running in 600 ppm water scales its heat exchanger in half the time that one in 200 ppm water would. A salt cell rated for five years may last two. The tile line crusts over in 12 to 18 months instead of the two to three years you would see in softer-water cities.

The practical result is that pool equipment in Phoenix wears out faster, chemistry drifts faster, and the margin between a clean pool and a scaled, cloudy one is narrower than anywhere else in the state. Weekly service is not optional here — it is the baseline.

What Every Weekly Visit Covers

The technician follows the same checklist every week. He skims the surface, empties the skimmer and pump baskets, brushes the walls, steps, and waterline tile, and vacuums the floor. Then he tests the water — free chlorine, pH, alkalinity, cyanuric acid, and calcium hardness — and adjusts whatever needs adjusting. He checks the filter pressure gauge, inspects the pump, and looks at every piece of visible equipment. If something is wrong, you hear about it the same day.

Chemicals are included in the monthly price. Chlorine, acid, stabilizer, and anything else your pool needs on a routine basis are part of what you pay. One number on your bill every month, nothing extra.

Year-Round Pool Season

Most Phoenix pools never close. The water stays warm enough to swim from March through November without a heater, and many homeowners heat through the winter and swim year round. That means twelve months of equipment run time, twelve months of chemistry drift, and twelve months of calcium building on every surface.

Monsoon season — mid-June through September — is the hardest stretch. Dust storms load the pool with fine particulate that clogs filters in a single pass, and the organic debris that follows a thunderstorm can turn water green within 48 hours. Summer highs above 115 degrees push water temperatures past 90, which doubles chlorine demand and accelerates algae growth. We adjust chemical dosing and visit focus by season so the pool stays ahead of whatever the weather throws at it.

Repairs and Equipment

When something breaks, we diagnose and fix it on site. Pumps, motors, filters, heaters, salt cells, lights, and plumbing leaks — we carry common parts on the truck and can usually finish the repair the same visit. If a part needs to be ordered, we tell you the lead time and come back to install it. Repairs are quoted separately from the monthly cleaning price, and you see the number before any work starts.

If your pump is at the end of its life and a motor swap costs more than half of a new variable-speed unit, we will tell you. A variable-speed pump cuts energy costs by 60 to 80 percent, and in a market where pools run year round, the savings are significant. Arizona also requires variable-speed pumps on new construction and certain replacements under the Department of Energy's 2021 rule.
