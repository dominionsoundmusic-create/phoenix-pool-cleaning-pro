Pool Equipment Repairs You Should Never DIY (And Why Phoenix Homeowners Call the Pros)

Owning a pool in Phoenix is one of life's great pleasures — until something breaks. With our blazing summers and near-constant pool use, equipment failures happen, and when they do, the temptation to fire up a YouTube tutorial and tackle the repair yourself is real. We get it. But some pool equipment repairs are not just tricky — they're genuinely dangerous, potentially expensive to get wrong, and in some cases, illegal to perform without a license in Arizona. Before you grab your wrench, here's an honest look at the pool repairs you should always leave to a certified pool professional.

1. Pool Pump Motor Replacement or Rewiring

Your pool pump is the heart of your entire circulation system, and when it starts making grinding noises or stops running altogether, it can feel like a straightforward fix. Swap the motor, done — right? Not quite.

Pool pump motors involve 240-volt electrical connections that can cause serious injury or death if handled incorrectly. Beyond the safety risk, improper wiring can damage your new motor immediately, void your warranty, or create a fire hazard inside your equipment pad. In Arizona, electrical work on pool equipment typically requires a licensed contractor under state electrical codes.

A professional pool technician in Phoenix will not only replace the motor safely but will also check for underlying issues — like a clogged impeller or failing capacitor — that may have caused the original failure. Fixing just the symptom without finding the root cause means you'll be back to square one within weeks.

2. Gas Pool Heater Repairs

If your pool heater isn't igniting, is producing a weak flame, or is throwing error codes, please step away from the unit. Gas heater repairs involve natural gas or propane lines, combustion systems, and heat exchangers — a combination that demands certified expertise.

A gas leak from a poorly resealed fitting doesn't just mean a broken heater. It means a potential explosion risk and carbon monoxide danger for your family. In the Phoenix metro area, gas appliance work must be performed by licensed professionals, and your utility company can actually flag unpermitted work during routine inspections.

Common gas heater issues like faulty thermostats, corroded burner trays, or failing pressure switches all look deceptively simple in repair videos — but each one requires calibration, proper testing equipment, and an understanding of how the full combustion system interacts. This is one repair where the cost of calling a professional is always worth it.

3. Pool Electrical System Work — Including Lights and GFCI Outlets

Pool lighting might seem like a basic electrical task, but underwater and near-water electrical systems are governed by strict National Electrical Code (NEC) requirements specifically because water and electricity are a deadly combination. Replacing a pool light niche, repairing bonding wires, or resetting GFCI breakers that keep tripping all require a thorough understanding of pool-specific electrical safety.

Improper bonding — the system that equalizes electrical potential around your pool — is one of the leading causes of electric shock drowning (ESD), a tragically underreported hazard. If your GFCI keeps tripping or you notice a tingling sensation in the pool water, stop using the pool immediately and call a licensed pool electrician.

For Phoenix homeowners, this also applies to adding new outlets near the equipment pad or running conduit for new equipment. Always pull the appropriate permits and use a licensed professional for any pool-related electrical work.

4. Pool Filter System Overhauls

Cleaning your filter cartridges? That's a perfectly reasonable DIY task. But disassembling a DE (diatomaceous earth) filter, repairing a cracked filter tank, or replacing multiport valves is a different story entirely.

Pool filters operate under significant pressure. A DE or sand filter tank that isn't properly reassembled can blow apart under normal operating pressure, sending heavy plastic components flying with enough force to cause serious injury. We've seen this happen — and it's not pretty.

Additionally, DE filters contain diatomaceous earth, a substance that requires careful handling to avoid respiratory damage during cleaning and recharging. Pool professionals have the right equipment and training to handle filter overhauls safely and to spot hairline cracks or worn o-rings that an untrained eye will miss.

5. Automated Pool System and Control Board Repairs

Many Phoenix homes now have automated pool systems — think Pentair IntelliTouch, Jandy AquaLink, or Hayward OmniLogic. These systems control your pump speeds, lighting, heater, and chemical dosing from a single panel or smartphone app. They're incredibly convenient, and incredibly complex.

When a control board fails or a communication error appears, the troubleshooting process involves reading circuit boards, checking actuators, testing relay outputs, and sometimes updating firmware. Getting this wrong doesn't just mean a non-functional feature — it can fry an expensive control board (often $500–$1,500 to replace) or cause connected equipment to run incorrectly, burning out pumps or heaters.

Authorized dealers and certified technicians for these systems have access to manufacturer diagnostic tools and training that the average homeowner simply doesn't. When your smart pool system goes dumb, call someone who speaks its language.

6. Pool Plumbing Leaks — Especially Underground Lines

A dripping fitting above ground might seem like a quick PVC glue fix, and sometimes it is. But if you're losing water consistently and can't identify the source, you may have a leak in your underground plumbing — and that's where DIY gets expensive fast.

Underground pool plumbing in Phoenix is often buried beneath concrete decking, pavers, or landscaping. Identifying the leak requires pressure testing, acoustic listening equipment, or tracer dye — professional tools that pinpoint the problem before any digging begins. Guessing at the location and cutting into the wrong spot can cost you thousands in unnecessary concrete work.

Even above-ground plumbing repairs need to be done with the right pipe schedule, primer, and cement for pool-rated pressure. A bad joint that fails again in a week wastes your time and money — and can cause water damage to your equipment pad or surrounding landscape.

Know When to Call a Pro — Your Pool (and Safety) Depend on It

There's no shame in DIY pool care for routine tasks — skimming, vacuuming, testing water chemistry, and replacing filter cartridges are all things a motivated homeowner can handle. But when it comes to electrical systems, gas equipment, pressurized tanks, automated controls, and underground leaks, the risks of a DIY mistake far outweigh the cost of hiring a licensed professional.

In Phoenix, where pools run nearly year-round and equipment is under constant stress from heat and heavy use, staying ahead of repairs with regular professional maintenance is almost always cheaper than emergency fixes. A certified pool technician can spot wear and potential failures during routine service visits — before they become costly breakdowns or safety hazards.
