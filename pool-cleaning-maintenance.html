What a Weekly Visit Looks Like

Every visit follows the same routine, in the same order, so nothing gets skipped. The technician skims the surface, empties the skimmer basket and the pump basket, brushes the walls, steps, and waterline tile, and vacuums the floor. Then he tests the water — free chlorine, pH, alkalinity, cyanuric acid, and calcium hardness — and adjusts whatever needs adjusting. He checks the filter pressure gauge, inspects the pump and any other visible equipment, and logs what he found and what he dosed.

The whole visit takes 20 to 40 minutes depending on the pool's size and condition. If something is wrong — a pump bearing going, a filter pressure climbing, a salt cell reading low — you hear about it the same day, not after it has turned into a bigger problem.

Why Weekly Matters More in Phoenix

A pool in Seattle can go two weeks between cleanings and stay reasonable. A pool in Phoenix cannot, and the reason is that this city hits pools harder than almost any other metro in the country.

The water

Phoenix tap water runs 300 to 900 parts per million calcium hardness depending on where in the Valley you are. The Colorado River and Salt River sources that feed the city carry mineral loads that are among the highest of any municipal supply in the United States. That calcium concentrates every day as the sun evaporates water off the surface, and it deposits on everything — tile, heater cores, salt cells, filter grids, and the inside of the pump. Weekly testing is the only way to track it and adjust before the saturation index tips into scaling territory.

The heat

Phoenix averages 107 days above 100 degrees and regularly breaks 115 in June and July. Water temperatures climb past 90, which doubles chlorine demand — the chlorine the pool needs to stay sanitized burns off faster in hot water. Without weekly dosing, a pool that tested fine on Monday can test zero chlorine by Thursday and show the first signs of algae by Saturday.

The dust

Desert dust is a daily fact, and monsoon season from mid-June through September makes it worse. A haboob can deposit enough fine particulate to cloud a pool overnight and load the filter in one pass. Weekly brushing and vacuuming keep that dust off the plaster before it stains, and weekly basket emptying keeps the skimmer flowing instead of choking.

Monsoon Season

Mid-June to the end of September is the hardest stretch of the year for pool maintenance in Phoenix. Storms hit fast, dump dust and organic debris, and raise humidity that feeds algae. Water temperature peaks, chlorine demand peaks, and the pool needs more attention than any other three-month window.

During monsoon season we increase chemical dosing and spend more time on debris removal. If a storm hits between your regular visits and you wake up to green water or a pool full of leaves, call and we will come out for an additional cleanup. On most service plans that extra visit is included — you should not have to pay twice because the weather was bad.

Salt Water Pools

Salt pools still need weekly service. The salt cell generates chlorine, but it does not brush the walls, skim the surface, balance pH, or manage calcium. We inspect the cell every visit for scale buildup and clean it when output drops. In Phoenix's hard water, salt cells scale faster than the manufacturer's timeline — a cell rated for five years may last two to three without regular descaling. Weekly inspection and cleaning extends the cell's life and saves you from a $1,200 replacement sooner than it should have come.
